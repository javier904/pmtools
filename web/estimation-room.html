7 Estimation Methods for Every Team Need

🃏

Fibonacci

The classic agile estimation sequence: 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20, 40, 100 plus ? (unsure) and coffee break cards. The golden standard for story point estimation used by most Scrum teams worldwide.

Best for: Story point estimation, Sprint Planning, Backlog Refinement

👕

T-Shirt Sizes

Quick relative sizing with XS, S, M, L, XL and XXL. Removes the pressure of assigning exact numbers and focuses on relative complexity. Perfect for initial backlog estimation and non-technical stakeholders.

Best for: Quick sizing, high-level estimation, cross-functional teams

📐

Three-Point (PERT)

Statistical estimation with Optimistic, Most Likely and Pessimistic values. The PERT formula (O + 4M + P) / 6 produces a weighted estimate. Includes standard deviation and variance calculation for risk quantification.

Best for: High-uncertainty items, risk assessment, project forecasting

🔵

Dot Voting

Silent prioritization where each participant allocates a fixed number of points (1-10, default 5) across options. Reveals team priorities without group discussion bias. Ideal for feature prioritization and roadmap planning.

Best for: Prioritization, feature selection, democratic decision-making

🪣

Bucket System

Affinity-based estimation that groups items into complexity buckets: XS, S, M, L and XL. Teams sort stories by relative complexity rather than precise estimates. Perfect for rapid estimation of large backlogs.

Best for: Large backlog estimation, quick sorting, initial project scoping

🖐️

Five Fingers

Rapid 1-to-5 voting for quick team consensus. Based on the fist-to-five technique used in agile ceremonies. Simple and fast — perfect for confidence checks, quick polls and lightweight estimation rounds.

Best for: Confidence votes, quick polls, daily standups, rapid consensus

🔢

Decimal (Free Input)

Free-form decimal input for precise estimates like 1.5, 2.75 or 3.25 hours. No predefined cards — each voter enters their own value. Ideal for time-based estimation and effort tracking in hours or days.

Best for: Time-based estimation, effort in hours/days, precise values

Frequently Asked Questions

What estimation methods are available in Keisen?

Keisen Estimation Room offers 7 estimation methods: Fibonacci (classic story point sequence 0-100 with ? and coffee break cards), T-Shirt Sizes (XS to XXL for quick relative sizing), Three-Point PERT (Optimistic + 4x Most Likely + Pessimistic / 6 with variance analysis), Dot Voting (silent point allocation for prioritization), Bucket System (affinity-based grouping by complexity), Five Fingers (rapid 1-5 voting for quick consensus), and Decimal (free-form input for precise estimates like 1.5 or 2.75).

Is the Estimation Room free?

Yes, Keisen Estimation Room is completely free. You can create estimation sessions, invite your team, vote in real-time and track statistics for all 7 estimation methods.

How does real-time voting work?

All team members vote simultaneously with hidden cards to prevent anchoring bias. Once everyone has voted, the facilitator reveals all cards at once (or auto-reveal can be enabled). The system then calculates statistics including mean, median, mode and consensus detection. Live presence tracking shows who is online and who has voted.

What is Three-Point (PERT) estimation?

Three-Point PERT is a statistical estimation technique where each voter provides three values: Optimistic (best case), Most Likely (expected), and Pessimistic (worst case). The PERT formula (O + 4M + P) / 6 produces a weighted estimate that accounts for uncertainty. Keisen also calculates standard deviation and variance to quantify estimation risk.

Can I use Estimation Room with a remote team?

Yes, Keisen is designed for both remote and co-located teams. It works in any browser with no software to install. Team members can join via invite link, vote in real-time, and see results instantly. Live presence tracking shows who is online, making it perfect for distributed agile teams.

What statistics are available after voting?

After votes are revealed, Keisen shows comprehensive statistics: average (mean), median, mode (most frequent vote), vote distribution chart, min/max range, and automatic consensus detection. For PERT estimation, it also shows aggregate PERT values, average standard deviation and total variance across all voters.

How do I get started with Keisen?

Getting started is quick and simple: sign in with your Google account and you are ready to go. Create an estimation session, choose one of 7 methods, add your user stories and invite your team. The entire setup takes less than a minute.

How is Keisen different from PlanITPoker, Scrumpoker-online or PointingPoker?

Keisen offers 7 estimation methods (most tools only support Fibonacci), built-in PERT three-point estimation with variance analysis, Dot Voting and Bucket System for advanced prioritization, real-time presence tracking, multi-role support (Facilitator, Voter, Observer), and direct export to Smart Todo and Agile Sprint backlog — all completely free.
